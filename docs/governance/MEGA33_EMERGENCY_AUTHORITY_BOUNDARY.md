# Mega Step 33 — Emergency Authority Boundary

Mega Step 33 establishes the launch baseline for emergency governance authority.

Emergency conditions do not create unlimited authority.

Any emergency authority recognized by Lumora must be:

1. attached to an authenticated actor;
2. explicitly delegated;
3. based on a declared and documented emergency;
4. necessary because ordinary process is insufficient;
5. narrow in scope;
6. proportionate to the emergency;
7. temporary;
8. subject to an explicit future sunset;
9. reviewable by a human process;
10. auditable;
11. compatible with remedy and appeal.

Emergency authority must not:

- suspend the constitutional fundamental-rights baseline;
- become permanent merely because an emergency occurred;
- create irreversible governance-rights mutations;
- silently enlarge permanent governance powers;
- bypass authentication or explicit delegation;
- remove review, explanation, remedy, or accountability.

The emergency boundary is therefore a temporary operational authorization boundary, not a new sovereignty layer, election system, token-voting system, treasury sovereignty mechanism, or private-beta activation mechanism.

Mega Steps 27–32 remain authoritative and are not weakened by this boundary.

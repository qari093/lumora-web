# Lumora Platform Constitution — Foundational Baseline v1.0.0

## Status

This document defines Lumora's canonical **foundational constitutional baseline** for the founder/private-beta stage.

It is a platform governance contract, not a claim that Lumora is a state, sovereign government, nation, or source of legal citizenship.

Within Lumora product language, **citizen means community member only**.

## 1. Irreducible baseline

Lumora's baseline protects:

- human dignity;
- equal treatment and non-discrimination;
- privacy;
- meaningful explanation for consequential platform decisions;
- access to a human-review path for consequential automated decisions;
- remedy and appeal;
- proportionality;
- data portability.

Lawful restrictions must be necessary, narrow, documented, proportionate, and reviewable. Dignity, remedy, and protection against arbitrary action remain baseline safeguards.

## 2. Authority boundary

Community roles do not create state authority or legal sovereignty.

Lumora remains a platform, service, company ecosystem subject to applicable law and the authority of its legally authorized corporate actors.

Governance authority must not be automatically derived from popularity, wealth, follower count, token holdings, or engagement metrics.

## 3. Community / corporate separation

Community governance may guide platform/community matters within delegated scope.

It does not replace:

- corporate fiduciary or statutory duties;
- legally required company decision-making;
- regulatory obligations;
- contractual obligations;
- judicial or governmental authority.

Where community preference conflicts with binding law or a non-delegable legal duty, the lawful obligation controls, with the conflict documented where disclosure is lawful and safe.

## 4. Amendment procedure

A constitutional amendment requires:

1. a new version identifier;
2. a documented reason;
3. an effective date;
4. a rights-impact review;
5. disclosure of relevant conflicts of interest;
6. a public change record where lawful and safe;
7. preservation of previous published versions.

A person or body whose own constitutional powers are affected must not possess a unilateral veto over the amendment solely because their powers are affected.

## 5. Constitutional history

Published constitutional versions are append-only records.

Sealed versions must use cryptographic digests and reference prior versions so silent rewriting can be detected.

Mega Step 27 establishes this requirement and canonical contract. Durable archive/storage enforcement may be activated at the appropriate maturity and persistence gate; this baseline does not falsely claim that a new governance database has already been deployed.

## 6. Transparency

Consequential governance and moderation decisions must be explainable and auditable to the degree appropriate to the affected person and authorized reviewers.

Transparency does not require publishing private personal data, sensitive evidence, security information, abuse intelligence, credentials, or protected legal material.

## 7. Governance–economy firewall

Fundamental rights cannot be purchased.

Money, Zencoin, revenue, follower count, engagement, or commercial status must not automatically grant constitutional authority.

Economic rewards and governance eligibility remain distinct concepts.

## 8. AI constitutional boundary

AI may assist with analysis and recommendations, but the constitutional baseline does not authorize AI to autonomously:

- impose irreversible permanent bans without a human-review path;
- make irreversible fund transfers;
- secretly alter governance rights;
- secretly manipulate reputation;
- remove remedy or appeal for consequential decisions.

Deeper AI-governance enforcement belongs to its designated maturity gate.
